01

The story

Why a recipe site should look like something you'd pick up for free next to the honey stall.

This is a personal collection, not a publication. It holds Babkallah (a babka braided like challah), hot honey chicken, a Zombie and microwave applesauce on the same shelf. That mix of high effort and low effort, of sweet and savoury and boozy, is how a homemade zine feels: eclectic, generous and a bit loud.

The site's name is a list of verbs, so the identity moves. Display type is printed twice, blue and pink, and the pink drum is always a little off, so every heading shows a purple overprint with coloured fringes. Tap a tab and the pink layer jolts out of register and snaps back. Hover a card and its halftone corner sizzles.

A brand made of ink, paper and print needs no food photography to look finished. And when photos do arrive, they get printed in the same two inks.

“The home page is the cover. The recipe page is the press sheet: loud on the outside, calm where your hands are covered in flour.”Design principle no. 1

Three rules

  • Pink is never text. It fails contrast (2.8:1). It's fills, shadows and the misregistered plate.
  • Shake the identity, not the reading. Motion happens on the ink layer only, never moves paragraphs, and stops completely with reduced motion.
  • Stickers tilt on the cover only. At most 1.2°, off below 360px wide and gone entirely on recipe pages.
02

Logo

A stacked sticker wordmark in condensed Bricolage: blue plate, pink plate knocked 3px out of register, and a giant halftone ampersand behind. Overlaps are drawn as a true overprint (purple), not a blend.

Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the cap height of the S on every side. The ampersand may break that zone, because it is meant to poke out.

Minimum size

Below 96px wide the stacked mark loses its ink traps: switch to the monogram. Below 20px, use a flat blue S.

Short and loud.Two to four words on buttons. Exclamation marks go on stickers, not in steps.
Recipes stay straight.Steps, amounts and temperatures are never cute. They're read with sticky hands.

10

How to adopt

One stylesheet, one font file and one header edit. No JavaScript changes.

  1. Copy the theme. Replace assets/style.css with style-guides/overprint/theme.css. It already holds every selector and state the JS uses.cp style-guides/overprint/theme.css assets/style.css
  2. Copy the font next to it. The theme loads ./fonts/bricolage.woff2 relative to the stylesheet. Keep the licence with it.mkdir -p assets/fonts cp style-guides/overprint/fonts/bricolage.woff2 style-guides/overprint/fonts/OFL.txt assets/fonts/
  3. Copy the marks. Use the monogram as the favicon, and regenerate icon-192.png / icon-512.png from it.cp style-guides/overprint/icon.svg assets/icon.svg cp style-guides/overprint/logo.svg assets/logo.svg
  4. Swap the header brand in index.html and recipe.html: replace the 🍸 .brand-mark span with the inline <svg class="brand-logo"> from preview/index.html. It is CSP-safe: paths only, coloured by theme.css classes. Keep the .brand-name span: the theme hides it visually and screen readers still read it.<a class="brand" href="./"> <svg class="brand-logo" viewBox="0 0 402.0 294.2" aria-hidden="true" focusable="false">…</svg> <span class="brand-name">Shaken, Stirred <span class="amp">&amp;</span> Sautéed</span> </a>
  5. Update the theme colour meta (and theme_color / background_color in manifest.webmanifest) to the paper.<meta name="theme-color" content="#F5F4F0" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)"> <meta name="theme-color" content="#15121A" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
  6. Bump the service-worker cache name in sw.js so installed phones pick up the new stylesheet and font.

If you skip step 4, the theme still works: the original emoji header turns into a stacked, misregistered type wordmark.
