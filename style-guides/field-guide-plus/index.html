Field Guide, richer

The kitchen herbarium from round two, now in three botanical inks. Every recipe is a specimen sheet, and every section is a family with its own ink, its own plate drawing and its own Latin name. Open a cocktail and the whole page turns juniper blue. The palette is still calm and the type is still easy to read at the stove. There is just more to look at.

01

Concept

A field guide sorts living things into families and shows each one as a plate: a fine-line drawing, a name and a small ruled label. This site works the same way. Baking, cooking and cocktails are three families. Each recipe is a specimen, numbered in the collection, labelled with its facts and drawn in its family's ink.

Round two's Field Guide got the calm and the readability right but read as a bit plain. This version keeps the paper, the label and the grotesk body text. It adds a display face with some warmth, three inks that colour-code the whole site, bigger family plates to navigate by, and more drawings.

Everything is flat: solid colour, crisp 1 to 2 px rules, no shadows, bevels, gradients, hatching or grain.

Principles
  • Colour says where you areOne custom property, --section, carries the family ink. Tabs, cards, badges and whole recipe pages read it.
  • Never colour aloneEach family also has its own drawing and name, and the three inks differ in lightness as well as hue.
  • Rich, not busyCharacter comes from type, drawings and colour. There is no trim, no ornament for its own sake and no texture.
  • Readable at arm's length17 px body text, AA contrast or better on every text use, and 38 px or larger tap targets.
02

What changed and why

“The Field Guide actually looks really pretty but is kind of simple as well.”

  • A display faceFraunces, a soft old-style serif at a sturdy weight, for titles and family names. It is warm without being fussy, and it has no hairlines. Body text stays in Schibsted Grotesk.
  • More drawingsThree family plates (wheat, bay laurel, coupe) and five ingredient specimens (mint, lemon, chili, cinnamon, coffee). They appear on tabs, cards, recipe headers, the empty state and here.
  • A fuller recipe sheetThere is now a family line in Latin, a two-drawing plate, a lettered specimen list (a, b, c), captions on each rule and a closing drawing. On a laptop, the list sits next to the method.

“I like the idea of things being color coded… Each recipe is then highlighted in that color too maybe.”

  • Three inksElderberry for baking, bay laurel for cooking and juniper for cocktails. Each comes as an ink, a wash and a page tint.
  • Everywhere it mattersThe tabs are filled with the family wash. Each card has a coloured spine and a stamp, and a recipe page takes on its family colour: title, rules, ticks, current step and the page itself.
  • Big family labels on topThis idea comes from round one's Overprint. The tabs are now large plates with a drawing, a big name and a count, so you can see and reach them at a glance.

“I don't like the 3D shading… or the hash marks.”

  • Flat onlyThere are no drop shadows anywhere, not even on cards or the sheet. Depth comes from paper on board and from rules.
  • No textureThe paper feel is two flat tones, a mounting board and a sheet. There is no grain, hatching or halftone.

Tin-Glaze had “too much trim”; Gilt Hour looked like a “fancy, tacky hotel”.

  • Borders that do a jobEach rule either groups something or carries the family colour. There are no frames on frames and no corner ornaments.
  • No gold, no creamThe paper is still cool green-grey, and the accent is a lime tape strip, not metal. Fraunces is used at text-friendly weights, never as a hairline display.
03

Section colours

Each family has three tones. The ink is for text, rules, ticks and solid fills. The wash sits behind ink on tabs, card spines and label heads. The paper tints the whole page when you are inside that family. Ratios are WCAG 2.1 contrast, computed from the hex values. Text on a solid ink fill is white in light mode and board-dark in dark mode, at 5.9:1 or better. Inside a family the sheet and its hairlines pick up a faint tint of the family too (most visible in dark mode, where a green-grey sheet would fight a berry or blue page): --baking-sheet, --cooking-sheet, --cocktails-sheet and matching -line tokens. Body text stays at 13.6:1 or better on every sheet.

Telling them apart without colour

The three inks differ in lightness as well as hue. In light mode elderberry is darkest, juniper is in the middle and bay laurel is lightest (L* 25, 36, 43), and the order holds in dark mode. Colour is never the only cue: every family also has its own drawing, and its name is always written out.

04

Logo

The mark is a herbarium sheet in miniature: one sprig, pressed and held down by a strip of lime tape. Its three leaf pairs are the three families, from the bottom up: elderberry, laurel and juniper. The leaves are filled so they still read at 16 px. The wordmark is set in Fraunces SemiBold with an italic ampersand. On a family page the ampersand takes that family's ink, and the header's tricolour rule becomes a single colour.

  • Don't outline the leaves or recolour them out of order.
  • Don't add a shadow, bevel or glow.
  • Don't set the wordmark in the grotesk or in all caps.
  • Don't put the mark on a busy photo. Use the sheet as its frame.

05

Neutral colour

The neutrals are botanical ink and graphite on green-grey paper. Anything not tied to a family, like the "All" tab, the sort control and the search field, uses plain ink. When "All" is chosen it shows the three inks together as a flat band along its foot. Lime appears only as the specimen tape and the logo's tape, and is never used for text. Focus rings are ink in light mode and lime in dark mode.

06

Type

Display · FrauncesAa&Fam. Culinaceae

A soft old-style serif with variable optical size, softness and weight. It is used at 450 to 650 with SOFT 100, so it reads as friendly and well-fed rather than formal, and its italic gives the Latin family names. It is used for titles, tabs, section heads, step numbers and ingredient letters.

Reading · Schibsted GroteskAa

Kept from round two because it reads clearly at the stove. It is used for ingredients, steps, notes, buttons and controls, with 17 px body text.

Label · IBM Plex MonoNo.01

Used for the herbarium label: field names, quantities, counts, accession numbers and captions. It always appears small, and usually in caps with tracking.

Plates and specimens

The drawings are single-weight 1.35 px line on a 48 px grid, with round caps and no fills, so they read as fine-line plates rather than icons. They are used as CSS masks, so each one takes whatever ink it is given. Interface glyphs (search, clock, tick) are drawn the same way.

Pairings on the recipe plate: wheat with cinnamon, laurel with chili, coupe with mint. The empty state uses cinnamon, chili and lemon in the three inks.

Voice and microcopy

The voice is that of a naturalist's notebook kept by someone who cooks. It is exact about quantities and cues, and a little dry and fond everywhere else. The Latin is a wink and appears only on the family line and in this guide. The interface itself speaks plain English.

Write cues as things you can see, smell or hear ("smooth and supple, no longer shiny"). Write waits as ranges ("5 to 10 min"). Give one instruction per step, and use no exclamation marks.

How to adopt

  1. Copy theme.css to assets/style.css. Copy fonts/ to assets/fonts/, and the nine drawings (plate-*.svg, spec-*.svg) to assets/. Every url("./…") in the theme then resolves next to the stylesheet. No JavaScript changes are needed.
  2. In index.html and recipe.html, replace the emoji .brand-mark span with the inline SVG from preview/index.html and add <span class="brand-sub">A kitchen field guide</span> after the name. 
    <meta name="theme-color" content="#e8ede6" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#0e1511" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
  3. Use logo.svg as assets/icon.svg. Re-export icon-192.png and icon-512.png from it on the sheet colour #FBFCF9, and set the manifest theme_color to #e8ede6.
  4. Add the new files to the service worker's cache list in sw.js and bump its version, so phones pick up the fonts and drawings offline.
  5. CSP needs no changes: all fonts and drawings are same-origin, glyphs are data: URIs, and there are no inline styles or scripts.
  6. To add a fourth family later, add --<name>, -wash and -paper tokens and one selector block under "Families", and draw a plate for it.
