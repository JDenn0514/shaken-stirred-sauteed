01

Concept

A kitchen ticket is one of the most legible documents ever designed. It is read at arm's length, in a hurry, with wet hands. It lists what you need, then what to do in order, and it gets marked up as the work gets done. That is exactly how a recipe is used at home.

So the site borrows the ticket's structure, not its costume. Recipes are paper slips on a plain grey counter. Facts read like a receipt, with dotted leaders. Steps get struck through when they are done, and the one you are on gets a highlighter. Everything else stays out of the way.

  • One idea. Paper tickets. No rail hardware, no stamps, no barcodes on every card.
  • Type does the work. Martian Mono, compressed caps for titles, an easy sentence-case mono for everything you read.
  • Two signals. A small category flag, and the highlighter on the current step. Selection is plain ink.
  • Calm by default. 1px lines, flat fills, short transitions, nothing that blinks.
02

What changed from round one, and why

You liked Order Up for the idea and the readable type, and found it a bit maximalist. So the idea stays and most of the effects go.

Kept

  • Paper tickets and Martian Mono
  • Dotted receipt leaders for time, yield and facts
  • Highlighter on the current step
  • Strike-through on done steps and ingredients
  • Category colours, now as a small flag
  • The 80 mm receipt print
  • Dark mode as the kitchen display

Changed

Background
Brushed steel with grain becomes a flat cool grey, #D3D7DA.
Barcodes
Gone from the cards. One small barcode closes the recipe ticket.
Torn edge
Bottom of each ticket only, 10 × 5 px teeth, no top tear on the sheet.
The rail
Removed. The logo keeps it as a single line.
Controls
Heavy 2px black keys become 1px outlines on paper. Only selection is filled.
Caps
Only recipe titles and tiny labels. Buttons, tabs, steps and descriptions are sentence case.
Slang
Cut to two touches: “Back to the pass” and the “Order up” sign-off.
Dark glow
Phosphor green only on the selected section and the current step.
Colour count
A recipe screen shows ink, one category flag and the highlighter. That's it.
03

Logo

The mark is a ticket hanging from one line, the last trace of the rail. On it: a small flag, a heavy compressed ampersand (the “&” that joins shaken, stirred and sautéed) and one receipt leader. It is drawn from Martian Mono's own ampersand, so mark and type match.

Shaken, Stirred & Sautéed
Shaken, Stirred & Sautéed
  • Clear space: the width of the ticket's flag on every side.
  • Minimum size: 24px tall for the mark, 15px cap height for the wordmark.
  • The flag is the only colour. On dark backgrounds the paper turns light grey and the ink turns near black.
  • Don't add the steel rail back, outline the mark, set the wordmark in another font or put it on a photo.

08

Voice and microcopy

Plain, short and kind, like a good cook leaving a note for the next shift. Say what to do and what to look for. Kitchen slang is a seasoning: two touches across the whole site, and never where it could confuse.

  • Sentence case everywhere you read. Caps only for titles and tiny labels.
  • Numbers as numerals, units spelled the way the recipe printed them.
  • Verbs first in steps: “Whisk”, “Fold”, “Rest”.

09

Photography

No photos are required; the tickets stand on type. When a recipe has one, it should look like a quick, honest picture taken at the counter, not a styled spread.

  • Overhead or 45°, in daylight, on a plain surface: steel, grey stone, a white plate.
  • Natural colour. No filters, no grayscale, no heavy grading.
  • One subject, cropped tight, with room around it. 16:10 on cards, 16:9 on the recipe.
  • Mid-process is welcome: the braid before baking, the shaker tin frosting.
  • No hands-holding-fork stock shots, no garnishes added for the camera, no props.

How to adopt

  1. Copy theme.css to assets/style.css, replacing the old file.
  2. Copy fonts/martian-mono-latin-standard-normal.woff2 to assets/fonts/. The @font-face rule uses ./fonts/, which resolves next to the stylesheet.
  3. Copy logo.svg to assets/icon.svg and export 192 and 512px PNGs from it for icon-192.png and icon-512.png.
  4. In index.html and recipe.html, replace the emoji .brand-mark span with the inline SVG from preview/index.html, and swap the single theme-color for the light and dark pair.
  5. Update manifest.webmanifest: theme_color and background_color to #D3D7DA.
  6. Add the font to the service worker's cache list in sw.js and bump its cache version.

Nothing else changes

No JavaScript or markup changes are needed. The theme works with the CSP as it stands (no inline styles, no external hosts), supports light and dark, safe-area insets, reduced motion, keyboard focus, and prints a recipe as an 80 mm receipt.
