01

Concept

A free zine from the market stall, now printed clean.

Overprint started as a riso zine: loud type, sticker cards, a bottle-cap sticker slapped on the cover. You liked its energy and, above all, the giant BAKING / COOKING / COCKTAILS labels you can see and hit from across the kitchen.

This version keeps the zine and takes the printing errors away. Every colour is a flat, solid ink. Every edge is a crisp ink outline. Nothing is doubled, dotted or shadowed. Nothing tilts except the cover sticker, and only by 4°.

The new idea is that each section owns an ink. Its big tab, every card in it and every recipe page in it are printed in that ink, so you know where you are before you read a word.

  • One ink per section. Pistachio bakes, tomato cooks, plum pours.
  • Ink is the only depth. Outlines and fills, never shadows.
  • Big where it counts. Section labels, recipe names, step numbers.
  • Yellow means now. The highlighter is saved for the step you're on.
02

What changed and why

Each change answers something you said about rounds one and two.

You said

"I liked how the original Overprint had the big Baking, Cooking, Cocktails labels on top."

So

The labels are still huge (up to 74 px on a laptop). On a phone they now sit in a 2 × 2 grid, so Cocktails is never cut off by a sideways scroll. Each one wears its ink in a band along the top.

You said

"I don't like the 3D text on either or the hash marks on the original."

So

No offset pink and blue fringe on headings. No halftone dots, grain or hatching. No hard offset block shadows on cards or buttons. Headings are one solid colour.

You said

"What if we tried the original Overprint in a different color scheme?"

So

Fluoro pink and riso blue are out. In come three food-coloured inks (pistachio, tomato and plum) with a deep midnight ink for text, all on bright white paper. Nothing is gold, cream or neon.

You said

"I like the idea of things being color coded… each recipe is then highlighted in that color too."

So

A recipe page takes on its section ink everywhere: the paper wash, the ink bar, the back sticker, the title, the facts band, the checkboxes, the step discs and the squiggles.

Round two said

"The Riso Ticket looks good but it's kind of boring." "The Field Guide… is kind of simple."

So

This is not a quiet version. The cover sticker, the big wordmark, the chunky outlines and the sticker cards all stay, so it keeps round one's density.

03

Logo

The name, stacked like a zine masthead, and an ampersand printed in all three inks.

Use it like this

  • Keep at least the height of the S clear on every side.
  • The bands always run pistachio, tomato, plum from top to bottom. That is the section order.
  • The mark is at least 24 px. Below that, use the plain tile.

Not like this

  • No offset or doubled copy behind the letters.
  • No drop shadows, gradients or dot fills.
  • Don't rotate it. Only the cover sticker gets a tilt.
04

Colour

Three section inks, one text ink, one highlighter. All ratios are WCAG 2.x contrast.

The section inks

Each ink sits at a different lightness (light, mid, dark), so the three stay distinct in greyscale and for red-green colour blindness. Every section ink has four roles: --section (the fill), --section-on (text on that fill), --section-ink (the ink as text on paper) and --section-tint (the page wash).

Warm the milk over low heat, swirling, just until lukewarm. The microwave works too; just don't overshoot, since hot milk kills yeast.

Why Bricolage. Round one's font was easy to read and full of character. Its condensed 800 weight makes the big labels fit on a phone without shrinking them. At its text optical size it opens up into a friendly, very readable body face. One variable file (131 KB, SIL OFL) covers everything.

Rules. Display type is always one solid colour, either ink or the section ink. Never add a second offset colour behind it. Labels are uppercase and tracked. Body copy is sentence case and never condensed. Quantities are bold, with tabular numerals.

09

Voice & microcopy

Talk like the friend at the stove: short, specific, a little cheeky, never cute at the expense of clear.

Verbs on the cover

make · bake · cook · pour

One verb per section, printed small above each giant label.

Empty search

nada.

Then one plain sentence with a way out: "Nothing matches that. Clear filters".

The step you're on

now

One word in the highlighter. Cues say what you'll see: "Foamy", "Smooth and supple, no longer shiny".

Colophon

printed in three inks on bright white paper

The sign-off at the bottom of every page.

  • Do: "Reset checkmarks", "Keep screen on", "Clear all"
  • Do: sentence case in body copy, uppercase only in labels
  • Do: name the section by its colour in conversation: "it's a plum one"
  • Don't: exclamation marks outside the cover sticker
  • Don't: "Yummy!", "Delish", or puns in buttons
  • Don't: use a section ink for anything outside its section
10

How to adopt

Five steps, no build tools, nothing for the JavaScript to learn.

  1. Copy theme.css over assets/style.css, and copy fonts/bricolage.woff2 to assets/fonts/. The @font-face points at ./fonts/.
  2. Copy logo.svg to assets/logo.svg and point the favicon link at it. If you keep the old emoji header, the theme swaps the emoji for this mark on its own.
  3. For the full masthead, replace the .brand-mark span in index.html and recipe.html with the inline <svg class="brand-logo"> from preview/index.html. It uses classes, not inline styles, so the CSP stays as it is.
  4. Set theme-color to #F6F6F1 (light) and #15161F (dark), and regenerate icon-192/512.png from logo.svg.
  5. Leave sw.js alone. It is network-first, so it picks up the new stylesheet straight away and caches the font and logo the first time they load.

Section colouring needs no markup changes. The theme reads .badge.<category> on cards, the pressed .tab[data-cat] on the home page and .back[href*="cat=…"] on recipe pages, using :has() (Safari 15.4+, Chrome 105+, Firefox 121+). Older browsers get the plain ink version.
