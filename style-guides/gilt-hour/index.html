I

A menu card for a very good evening

The Corpse Reviver No. 2 on this site is credited, quite properly, to Harry Craddock, and its notes worry about Kina Lillet versus Cocchi Americano. Whoever keeps this collection loves the classic cocktail canon. Shaken and stirred are words from the 1930s cocktail age, so the brand goes back to where they began: the American Bar at the Savoy, Gilbert Rumbold’s Deco drawings in the Savoy Cocktail Book, lacquered navy, gold leaf and sunburst fans.

The site has no photographs yet, so ornament and type carry the beauty on their own. Every page opens like an elevator door, with gilt rays rising behind the title. Everything sits on one symmetrical axis. The cards are lacquer panels in stepped gilt frames. The pavlova, the crémeux and the babka arrive on the same gilded trolley as the Painkiller.

The ceremony stops where the cooking starts. Ingredients and steps stay calm and large enough to read at arm’s length with wet hands. Ornament appears only to mark the step you are on.

Mood

Dark by default, symmetrical, airy and frankly glamorous.

Material

Lacquer, gold leaf, engraved paper, a hairline of brass.

Never

Kitsch, flapper costume, faux-distressed texture or clutter on the working page.

House rules

  • Write counts out in words up to ninety-nine; above that, use numerals (“112 receipts”).
  • Steps stay imperative and literal: “Shake hard.” The wink lives in labels, empty states and the colophon.
  • Use the classic spelling where the canon does: coupe, absinthe, Curaçao.
  • Never use “yummy”, “easy-peasy” or exclamation marks. The maître d’ never shouts.

The scripts in assets/ write these strings, so the theme alone cannot change them. The right-hand column is the copy to adopt whenever home.js and recipe.js are next edited.

IX

Photography & illustration

For the day the recipes get pictures. Shoot them like a bar at night: one warm key light, deep navy shadows, gilt and glass catching the light.

Cocktails · glass on axis, backlit
Cooking · low angle, steam in the key
Baking · overhead, on dark marble
  • Light: a single tungsten-warm source from the side or behind, around 3200K, with shadows that fall into navy and never grey.
  • Composition: symmetrical and centred on the vertical axis, like the type. Coupes stand dead centre and plates sit square.
  • Props: brass, cut crystal, linen, dark marble, lacquer trays. No rustic wood, slate or mason jars.
  • Crops: 4:3 on cards and 3:2 for the recipe hero, framed by the double gilt hairline the theme already draws.
  • Grade: slightly lifted blacks toward #0B1020, warm highlights toward #F0D493, and saturation held about 10% below natural.
  • Illustration: if drawn, use single-weight gilt line work in the Rumbold manner: elongated, geometric and frankly decorative, never cartoon.

XI

How to adopt

Four small moves. Nothing in recipes/ or the scripts changes.

  1. Copy the theme and its fonts into assets/:
    • style-guides/gilt-hour/theme.css → assets/style.css (replace it)
    • style-guides/gilt-hour/fonts/ → assets/fonts/ (three .woff2 files)
    • style-guides/gilt-hour/icon.svg → assets/icon.svg
    • style-guides/gilt-hour/monogram.svg → assets/monogram.svg (only if you use the <img> header below)

    theme.css loads its fonts from ./fonts/, which resolves to assets/fonts/ once it lives at assets/style.css.

  2. In index.html and recipe.html, replace the brand inside <header class="site-header">: 
    <a class="brand" href="./">
  <svg class="brand-mark" viewBox="0 0 120 128"
       aria-hidden="true" focusable="false">
    <!-- paste the <defs>, .gh-line and .gh-fill paths from
         style-guides/gilt-hour/preview/index.html -->
  </svg>
  <span class="brand-name">
    <span class="brand-line">Shaken<span
      class="brand-sep" aria-hidden="true"></span><span
      class="visually-hidden">, </span>Stirred</span>
    <span class="brand-script"><span
      class="amp">&amp;</span> Sautéed</span>
  </span>
</a>

    The inline SVG is coloured by the theme (.gh-line, .gh-fill), so it turns deep gilt on the paper card. <img class="brand-mark" src="assets/monogram.svg" alt=""> also works; the monogram file switches between lacquer and paper colours on its own.

  3. Swap the theme colour so the phone’s browser chrome matches: 
    <meta name="theme-color" content="#0B1020"
      media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#F6F3EC"
      media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">
  4. Optional: regenerate icon-192.png and icon-512.png from icon.svg, set background_color and theme_color in manifest.webmanifest to #0B1020, add assets/fonts/* to the service-worker cache, and adopt the microcopy in section VIII.

The theme keeps to the site’s Content-Security-Policy: no inline styles, no external hosts, and all ornament comes from data-URI SVGs. It honours prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-color-scheme, safe-area insets and print.
