Brand direction · Order Up · Sans · round 3

Order up.

The ticket rail you liked, flat, quieter in colour and louder in type, with one colour per station.

Every recipe is still an order ticket hanging on the rail at the pass. The rail is now a flat bar and the tickets are flat white paper with a crisp torn edge. The type is Archivo, an industrial grotesk, in place of Martian Mono. Baking, cooking and cocktails each have their own colour, and a recipe page takes on its station's colour from the band at the top to the highlighter on the step you're on.

01

What changed and why

Round two went too quiet. This round keeps round one's character and density and fixes the things you named.

Heard · round 2
“I like the older Order Up better than the quieter version.”

Kept: the tickets on the rail, torn edges, the ticket number and station on every ticket, dotted receipt leaders, barcodes, the big station tabs with counts, the highlighted step, struck-through steps with a SENT stamp, the 80 mm print and the kitchen calls.

Heard · round 2
“I don't like the 3D shading from the original.”

Removed: the brushed-steel page, the chrome rail gradient, the drop shadows under the tickets and the bevelled keys. The rail is a flat ink bar, tickets are flat paper with a 1 px hairline, and buttons are flat with a 1.5 px border.

Heard · round 2
“Part of the issue was the colors and the font. What if we tried more muted colors and a different font?”

Changed: the primary yellow, red and blue on pure black are now a fog counter, white tickets and ink-navy type. Martian Mono is now Archivo: condensed capitals for names, a plain grotesk at normal width for reading. Mono is kept only for tiny ticket numbers.

Heard · round 2
“I like the idea of things being color coded… each recipe highlighted in that color too.”

Added: a colour for each station, with apricot for baking, olive for cooking and plum for cocktails. It is used on the tabs, the ticket bands and the badges. A recipe page takes its station's colour on the band, title, checkboxes, rules, stamp, step highlighter and the page behind the ticket.

02

Station colours

Each station has four values: a fill, a text colour for use on the fill, an ink for text on paper, and a pastel highlighter (wash). The three fills are spread far apart in lightness as well as hue, so they still read as different in greyscale and for colour-blind cooks. An icon and a station name always go with the colour.

Greyscale check · same fills, colour removed

The fills step from light to mid to dark (L* 76, 46 and 34 in light mode, and 78, 62 and 45 in dark), so they stay apart without hue. In a simulation of deuteranopia, apricot and olive both read as yellowish, but they are 30 L* apart. Plum reads as blue-grey.

Where the colour goes
  • Home Tab flag and selected tab, ticket band and icon, badge, and the count chip on a filter. When you pick a station tab, the active chips, the filter sheet's band, the “All day” tag and the page tint all switch to it.
  • Recipe The back link becomes the ticket's station band. It also colours the title, section rules, group markers, checked boxes, strike lines, the SENT stamp, the step highlighter, the FIRING tag, the “Look for” cues and a pale wash of the page behind the ticket.
  • Never as body text on the fill colour, except the text-on-fill pairs above. Components and badges like “Component” stay neutral ink.
  • How One custom property, --section (plus -on, -ink and -wash), is set by :has() on .tab[data-cat], .card:has(.badge.baking), body:has(.tab[aria-pressed=true]) and .recipe:has(> .back[href*="cat=baking"]).

04

Logo

The SS&S ticket still hangs from the rail, but the rail is flat and the ticket carries three station flags. The lettering is outlined from Archivo at wdth 62 and wght 800, so the SVGs need no font. The barcode is Code 39 for *SSS*.

  • Keep the flags in order: bake, line, bar, from left to right, as on the tabs.
  • Keep the rail level. Tickets can swing on hover, but the rail never moves.
  • No shading, chrome or shadows, on the mark or anywhere else.
  • No emoji as a logo. The 🍸 in the header is retired.
05

Type

Archivo, a grotesk from Omnibus-Type in the tradition of kitchen signage and order printers. One variable file (wdth 62–125, wght 100–900, 88 KB) gives both the double-height names and the calm body copy, the way Martian Mono's width axis did. Overpass Mono, a monospace cut of the Highway Gothic road-sign face, is used only for tiny ticket numbers and calls. Both are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 and self-hosted.

Rules

Double-height for names. The brand, h1, card titles, section heads, counts and step numbers use wdth 62 and wght 800 in capitals, with line-height 0.9 to 0.94. They may wrap.

Normal width for reading. Ledes, steps and ingredients use wdth 100 at 16 to 17 px, line-height 1.45 to 1.55, in sentence case. A grotesk at normal width reads far more easily on a phone than a narrowed mono did.

Tracked capitals for labels. dt, filter keys, tabs and badges are 11 to 13 px, wght 600 to 700, with 0.06 to 0.1em tracking.

Mono only for the printer. Ticket numbers, kickers like “*** The bar ***” and the sign-off are in Overpass Mono at 10 to 11.5 px. Mono is never used for anything you read to cook.

Tabular figures everywhere, so grams and ounces line up.

Voice & microcopy

Short line calls, warm underneath. Kitchen jargon is always a small kicker beside the plain words, never a replacement, so a screen reader and a first-time guest both get “Done”, “Clear all” and “Keep screen on”.

Call it short

Two or three words in capitals, with no exclamation marks. The kitchen is loud, so let the type do the shouting.

Sentence case to cook by

Steps, notes and ledes stay in calm sentence case. Only names and calls are set in capitals.

Warm underneath

“Nothing on the rail” is a shrug, not an error. Never blame the cook.

How to adopt

No build step and no JavaScript changes. Copy three things, then edit the header in two HTML files.

  1. Copy style-guides/order-up-sans/theme.css over assets/style.css.
  2. Copy style-guides/order-up-sans/fonts/ to assets/fonts/ (two woff2 files, 110 KB together). theme.css loads ./fonts/… relative to itself.
  3. Copy logo.svg to assets/icon.svg for the favicon, and re-export icon-192.png and icon-512.png from it.
  4. In index.html and recipe.html, replace the 🍸 brand-mark span with the inline SVG and update theme-color (right).
  5. In manifest.webmanifest, set theme_color to #1D2836 and background_color to #E4EAEE. To use the fonts offline, add the two woff2 files to the service worker's cache list.
