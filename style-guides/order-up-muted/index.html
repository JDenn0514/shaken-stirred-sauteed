Brand direction · Round three · Order Up, muted

Orderup.

Same rail. Flat paper. Three stations, three colors.

Round one's Order Up, rebuilt from the same parts: tickets hanging on the rail, torn edges, ticket numbers, receipt leaders, a highlighter on the step you're on and a stamp on the ones you've sent. The chrome, the shading and the loud primaries are gone. The ink is charcoal, the counter is putty, the type is softer, and every recipe now wears its station's color, from the tab on the home page to the last rule on the ticket.

What changed and why

Every change answers something you said about rounds one and two.

I like the older Order Up better than the quieter version.

Round one's layout and density are back: tickets hang on a rail with torn bottoms, each has a number and a station, times and yields run on dotted receipt leaders, barcodes close the ticket, and the station tabs are big again with big counts. The quiet version's plain boxes are gone.

I don't like the 3D shading from the original.

Everything is flat. The chrome rail is now a flat 5 px bar with a simple clip, the tickets are flat paper with a crisp torn edge, and the buttons are outlined, not bevelled. There's no brushed-steel texture, no grain and not a single drop shadow in the stylesheet.

Part of the issue with the original was the colors… what if we tried more muted colors?

The steel counter is now warm putty, the paper is off-white and the ink is charcoal, not black. Highlighter yellow, alarm red and blue became ochre, brick and slate. These are enamel and kitchen-paper colors that sit together quietly but still read at a glance.

…and the font actually. A potentially different font?

Martian Mono is out. Body text is Red Hat Mono, which keeps the ticket feel but is rounder, lighter and easier to read in long steps. Titles and big numbers use Barlow Condensed, which gives the "double-height print" look without the weight. Six pairings were tested at phone size (see Type).

I liked how the original Overprint had the big Baking, Cooking, Cocktails labels on top… easy to see and navigate.

The station tabs are the biggest thing under the search: one ticket per station, each with its color flag, its name and a 38 px count. The selected tab fills with its color, and the rest of the page follows it.

I like the idea of things being color coded… each recipe is then highlighted in that color.

Each station has its own color on the tab, the ticket header, the badge and the whole recipe page. On a recipe, the header band, title, rules, checkboxes, highlighter, FIRING tag, SENT stamp, page tint and the stripe under the site header all switch to the recipe's station. The three colors step from light to dark, so they stay distinct for color-blind readers too.

The Riso Ticket… kind of boring. The Field Guide… kind of simple.

The ornament stays, but each piece does a job: the stripe shows the stations, the ticket number counts the rail, the leaders line up the numbers, the stamp says "done" and the highlighter says "you are here".

01

Concept

Why a ticket and not a cookbook.

Shaken, stirred, sautéed. Those are calls made at a pass, and the bake station, the line and the bar all run on tickets.

The recipes are already written like line specs: grams beside cups, ounces beside millilitres, a wait time and a cue on each step. So the site works like a kitchen's expediting rail. The home page is the rail of open tickets. A recipe is one ticket, read top to bottom while you fire it. Filters print out of the bottom of the screen like a slip from the printer.

Round three keeps that story but tells it more quietly. The paper is flat, the rail is a line, and the colors are the ones you'd find on enamel pans and order pads. Color does a job now, too: it tells you which station you're in. At night, the rail turns into a calm kitchen display screen with dark panels, soft station colors and nothing that glows.

Distinct without hue

BakeLineBar
BakeLineBar

The same three fills, then the same three in grayscale. In light mode they step from light (relative luminance 0.40) to mid (0.14) to dark (0.06), so the colors stay apart without hue, and under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia. Color is never the only signal: every station also has its name on the tab and ticket header, an icon on wider screens, and the words "Bake station", "The line" or "The bar" on the recipe.

04

Type

A softer mono to read by, a condensed sans to shout the order.

Titles · Barlow Condensed 600 / 700 · SIL OFL

Recipe titles, card titles, section heads, tab counts, step numbers. It's tall and narrow like double-height thermal print, so long names like "Italian Wedding Soup with Acini di Pepe" still fit on a phone in two lines. It's lighter in color than Martian Mono's heavy black.

Reading · Red Hat Mono 300–700 (variable) · SIL OFL

113 g · 1 stick butter, room temp

Ingredients, steps, labels, leaders. It's still monospaced, so quantities line up and the ticket voice survives, but the letters are open and round with soft terminals, and it reads at 16 px for a whole method without fatigue. Body is set at weight 430 on the variable axis.

Gutter 16 px plus safe-area insets. Radius 3 px on keys and fields, 0 everywhere else. Lines are 1.5 px. Tap targets are at least 44 px.

Line icons are drawn on a 24 grid with 2 px square-capped strokes. They're stored as CSS masks, so they take any color and stay inside the CSP. The site's emoji are hidden by the theme and replaced with these icons or words.

How to adopt

One stylesheet, three font files, one icon. No JavaScript changes.

  1. Copy the theme and fontsCopy theme.css over assets/style.css, and copy the fonts/ folder to assets/fonts/. The @font-face URLs are relative (./fonts/…), so they keep working. 
    cp style-guides/order-up-muted/theme.css assets/style.css
mkdir -p assets/fonts && cp style-guides/order-up-muted/fonts/*.woff2 assets/fonts/
  2. Swap the header markIn index.html and recipe.html, replace the emoji <span class="brand-mark"> with the inline <svg class="brand-mark"> from preview/index.html. Its colors come from the theme through classes, so it works under style-src 'self'.
  3. Icon and theme colorCopy logo.svg to assets/icon.svg and re-export icon-192.png and icon-512.png from it. Set <meta name="theme-color"> to #2B2A27 (light) and #0F0F0E (dark), and set the manifest's theme_color to #2B2A27 and background_color to #D9D4CB.
  4. Offline cacheAdd the three assets/fonts/*.woff2 files to the precache list in sw.js so the type is there offline.
  5. Nothing elseThe JavaScript and the recipe JSON don't change. The section colors come from markup the site already renders (.tab[data-cat], .badge.<category>, and the back link's ?cat=).
