Every recipe is a specimen sheet with a drawing of its own, every section is a family with its own ink, and after dark the cabinet goes graphite with the inks lifted but still saturated. Palette: Order Up inks

This is the final Field Guide with one change: its colour. You asked whether the Field Guide could be less pastelly, closer to Order Up. Layout, type, drawings, icons, components and behaviour are exactly as they were; only tokens and hex values moved.

The three families now take Order Up’s station colours. Baking is ochre, cooking is brick and cocktails is slate. Each is tuned into a text-safe Field Guide ink (ochre #8E5A08, brick #87351F, slate #213E53), and Order Up’s own station colours (#D29B3C, #A9462E, #2D4A5C) become the solid fills for chosen tabs, options and the family cards.

By day the pink, green and blue washes are gone. Washes are now a whisper, every recipe page sits on the same plain green-grey paper, and the family shows up in confident ink: the header rule, title, drawing, label edge, ticks, strikes and the current step. After dark the board turns to Order Up’s graphite with a faint green lean, and the inks are lifted, saturated mid-tones (#E4B45A, #D96B4D, #7AADD5) instead of pale rose, sage and sky.

What moved
  • InksElderberry, bay laurel and juniper become ochre, brick and slate. They step light, mid and dark by day, so they stay apart in greyscale too.
  • GroundsWashes and recipe pages lose almost all their tint. The board is warmed a touch toward Order Up’s putty (#E6E8E0).
  • FillsA chosen ochre tab is a bright ochre slab lettered in ink. Brick and slate fills are lettered in white.
  • TapeThe lime tape is kept but cooled and softened (#BFCF6C), so it no longer fights the ochre.
  • After darkGraphite board #131715, deep fills, and saturated inks at 4.7:1 or better on every ground they touch.
01

Concept

A field guide sorts living things into families and shows each one as a plate: a fine-line drawing, a name and a small ruled label. This site works the same way. Baking, cooking and cocktails are three families. Each recipe is a specimen: numbered in the collection, labelled with its facts, drawn in its family's ink, and now given its own drawing, so a babka looks like a loaf and a Painkiller looks like a highball.

This is the final round of the Field Guide. It keeps everything you liked (the calm green-grey paper, the flat tonal shading of tabs and cards, the big family tabs, the colour coding and the specimen label) and finishes the three things that were still open: a drawing for every recipe, a dark mode designed for cooking at night, and a clearer strike on finished lines.

Everything is flat: solid colour, crisp 1 to 2 px rules, no shadows, bevels, gradients, hatching or grain.

Principles
  • Colour says where you areOne custom property, --section, carries the family ink. Tabs, cards, badges and whole recipe pages read it.
  • A drawing says what it isEach recipe's drawing is the fastest way to spot it in the list, before you read a word.
  • Readable at arm's lengthSchibsted Grotesk for every line you read, at 17 px. Fraunces only for titles. AA contrast or better in both modes.
  • Rich, not busyCharacter comes from type, drawings and colour, not from trim, texture or ornament.
02

What changed and why

“I kind of like the shading and the idea of the icons.”

  • 29 recipe drawingsDrawn in the same hand as the family plates: one line, round ends, no fills. They are simpler than the plates so that they still read at 28 to 40 px. The line gets finer automatically when a drawing is shown large.
  • On every cardThe drawing heads each card's tinted spine in the family ink, where the generic family plate used to be. You can scan the list by shape: bowls, glasses, loaves.
  • Large on the recipeThe recipe's own drawing replaces the family plate in the header, large and alone in the family ink. (An earlier pairing with a small ingredient specimen was dropped: at phone size it read as a stray paper clip.)
  • Plates kept where they belongThe wheat, laurel and coupe plates still mark the three big tabs, and the empty state still shows three specimens.

Dark mode on the phone, cooking at night: it has to be designed, not inverted.

  • The cabinet after darkA graphite board with a faint green lean (#131715, never pure black) with sheets lifted one step (#1B201E). Cards and labels read as paper laid on the board, using tone alone and no shadow.
  • Inks lifted, calmlySaturated mid-tone ochre, brick and slate, at 4.8 to 8.6:1 on the sheet. Big fills (a chosen tab, a stamp) use a deep tone of the ink instead, so no bright slab lights up the kitchen.
  • Soft-ink drawingsDrawings mix 18% page ink into the family ink, so they read as soft pencil rather than neon.
  • No harsh whiteBody text is a soft green-white at 12.8:1. The current step is a tinted panel with an ink edge and a “Now” tab.

The crossed-out done lines are really helpful.

  • A clear strikeDone steps and checked ingredients are struck through with a 2 px line in the family ink. It is clear at a glance from across the counter.
  • Still readableThe struck text drops to graphite (6.3:1 or better on a recipe page by day, 6.4:1 after dark), so you can still read back what you did.
  • Done steps get a filled boxThe step number becomes a filled family box with a tick. The next open step becomes the current step.

Keep the Latin light and charming, but nothing twee.

  • Two names kept, one changedPistoriaceae (pistor, baker) and Culinaceae (culina, kitchen) stay. Miscellaceae read like “miscellaneous”, so cocktails become Mixtaceae (mixtus, mixed).
  • Once per pageThe Latin appears only as the small italic family line above a recipe title. Tabs, badges and buttons stay in plain English.
  • Plainer labels“Herbarium of Shaken, Stirred & Sautéed” became “Specimen label”, and “Annotations” became “Field notes”. “Specimen list” and “Procedure” stay, because they are short and clear.
  • Type: less monoSchibsted Grotesk for every line you read, now including ingredient quantities and wait times, which were in mono. Fraunces is used only for titles and the family line.
03

Recipe drawings

There are 29 drawings, one for every kind of recipe in the collection. Each is drawn on the plates' 48-unit grid, with a single line, round caps and joins, no fills, and one level of detail. Glassware is drawn by its true silhouette: a highball is tall and straight, a coupe is a shallow wide bowl, a Nick & Nora is a small deep bell, and a martini is a V.

The line is 2.1 units at icon sizes (about 1.4 px at 32 px). Each SVG carries a small media query that thins the line to 1.45 units from 56 px, 1.15 from 96 px and 0.95 from 150 px. As a result, the big header drawing has the same fine line as the family plates. The drawings are shown as CSS masks, so each takes its family ink (a little softer after dark).

Mapping rules, in order
  1. The recipe's own "icon"If a recipe file names one of the 29 keys, that wins.
  2. The chosen drawingEach current recipe has a hand-picked key (the per-recipe list in icons.js, shown under each drawing below).
  3. Cocktails: the glassglass containing nick/nora → nick-nora; tiki → tiki-mug; highball or collins → highball; coupe → coupe; martini → martini; rocks or old fashioned → rocks. Components (syrups) → bottle, and anything else → rocks.
  4. Baking: the typecakes → cake; cookies-bars → bars; pies-tarts → tart; breads and pastry → loaf; breakfast-bakes → donut; fillings-creams → jar.
  5. Cooking: the coursesoup → soup; salad → salad; sauce → sauce; base → pasta; drink → mug; protein → chicken; side → greens; full-meal → rice-bowl; sweet → apple; breakfast → loaf.
  6. Last resortloaf, soup or rocks by family.

Section colours

Each family has an ink for text, rules, ticks, strikes and drawings, a wash that sits behind ink on tabs, spines and label heads, a paper for the whole page inside the family, and a fill for chosen states. By day the fill is Order Up’s station colour: bright ochre lettered in botanical ink, brick and slate lettered in white. Washes and papers are only a whisper, so the colour comes from the ink. After dark the fill is a deep tone lettered in the ink. Ratios are WCAG 2.1 contrast computed from the hex values. Every pairing used as text passes AA, and many pass AAA.

Telling them apart without colour

The inks differ in lightness as well as hue. By day they step light, mid and dark: ochre, then brick, then slate, and the fills keep the same order (bright ochre, mid brick, dark slate). After dark ochre is lightest, then slate, then brick, each a clear step apart, so a red-green colour-blind reader still sees three different tones. Colour is never the only cue: the family name is always written out, each tab has its plate, and each card has its recipe's drawing.

05

After dark

The herbarium cabinet at night, with Order Up’s graphite-kitchen calm. The board is graphite with a faint green lean and the sheets are lifted one step, so cards, labels and the option sheet read as paper laid on a dark board, using flat tone alone. The inks are lifted to saturated mid-tones, not pastels and not neon, and nothing is pure white. These specimens are always shown dark, whatever your phone is set to.

  • Body text #DFE4DC on #1B201E, 12.8:1. Never pure white on pure black.
  • Large fills (a chosen tab, a stamp, an option) use the deep -fill tone, not the lifted ink.
  • Drawings are 82% ink and 18% page ink.
  • The current step is the only tinted panel in the method.

Logo

The mark is a herbarium sheet in miniature: one sprig, pressed and held down by a strip of lime tape. Its three leaf pairs are the three families, from the bottom up: ochre, brick and slate. The leaves are filled so they still read at 16 px. The wordmark is set in Fraunces SemiBold with an italic ampersand. On a family page the ampersand takes that family's ink, and the header's tricolour rule becomes a single colour. After dark the sheet becomes the lifted graphite and the leaves take the lifted inks.

  • Don't outline the leaves or recolour them out of order.
  • Don't add a shadow, bevel or glow.
  • Don't set the wordmark in the grotesk or in all caps.
  • Don't put the mark on a busy photo. Use the sheet as its frame.

Neutral colour

The neutrals are botanical ink and graphite on green-grey paper by day (the board warmed a touch toward Order Up’s putty), and soft green-white on graphite by night. Anything not tied to a family (the “All” tab, sort, search) uses plain ink. Lime appears only as tape, softened so it sits beside ochre, and is never used for text. Focus rings are ink by day and pale lime by night.

Type

Titles · FrauncesAa&Fam. Culinaceae

A soft old-style serif, set at 600 with SOFT 100, so it reads as warm and well-fed rather than formal. It has no hairlines. It is used only for titles: the wordmark, tabs, card and recipe titles, section heads, and the italic family line.

Reading · Schibsted GroteskAa

Every line you read while cooking is set in this: ingredients, steps, notes, ledes, buttons. It is open, sturdy and calm at 17 px, and its figures are clear for quantities and temperatures. You found monospace hard to read, so mono never carries a sentence.

Label · IBM Plex MonoNo.01

Used only for the herbarium label: small caps for field names, counts, accession numbers and captions. Quantities and wait times are in the grotesk, because you read them mid-stir. It is never used for prose.

Voice and microcopy

The voice is that of a naturalist's notebook kept by someone who cooks: exact about quantities and cues, and a little dry and fond everywhere else. The Latin is a wink. It appears once per recipe, in small italic graphite above the title, and nowhere in the controls.

Write cues as things you can see, smell or hear (“smooth and supple, no longer shiny”). Write waits as ranges (“5 to 10 min”). Give one instruction per step, and use no exclamation marks.

How to adopt

  1. Theme and fonts. Copy theme.css to assets/style.css. Copy fonts/ to assets/fonts/, the drawings (plate-*.svg, spec-*.svg) to assets/, and icons/ to assets/icons/. Every url("./…") in the theme then resolves next to the stylesheet.
  2. Header. In index.html and recipe.html, replace the emoji .brand-mark span with the inline SVG from preview/index.html, add <span class="brand-sub">A kitchen field guide</span> after the name, and set the theme colour per mode: 
    <meta name="theme-color" content="#e6e8e0" media="(prefers-color-scheme: light)">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#131715" media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)">
  3. Icons in the data. Move iconFor() and its tables from style-guides/_shared/icons.js into scripts/build-index.mjs. Write the result into each index entry as icon, and into the recipe JSON the page loads (or let recipe.js look it up). A recipe can still set its own "icon": "coupe", which wins. 
    // build-index.mjs, in index.push({ … })
icon: iconFor({ ...r, slug, facets }),
  4. Icons in the pages. Two small changes render the span the theme styles: 
    // home.js, card(r)
<a class="card" href="…" ${r.icon ? `data-icon="${esc(r.icon)}"` : ""}>
  <div class="card-body">
    ${r.icon ? `<span class="card-icon" aria-hidden="true"></span>` : ""}

// recipe.js, render(r) — before <h1>
${r.icon ? `<span class="recipe-icon" aria-hidden="true"></span>` : ""}
// and after setting innerHTML:
if (r.icon) root.dataset.icon = r.icon;
    Then drop icons.js. Cards without an icon fall back to their family plate.
  5. Logo and offline. Use logo.svg as assets/icon.svg. Re-export icon-192.png and icon-512.png on #FBFBF8, and set the manifest theme_color to #e6e8e0. Add the fonts, plates and icons to the cache list in sw.js and bump its version.
  6. CSP. Nothing changes: fonts and drawings are same-origin, glyphs are data: URIs, and there are no inline styles or scripts. The small <style> inside each icon SVG lives in the image, not the page.
  7. Later. To add a recipe type, draw a 48-unit icon in the same hand (single 2.1 line, round ends, no fills), add one [data-icon] line to the theme, and add the key to the mapping.
